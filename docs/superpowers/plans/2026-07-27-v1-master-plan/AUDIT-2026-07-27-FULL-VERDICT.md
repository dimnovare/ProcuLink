## 1. Executive verdict

**Partially — with conditions, and one condition is severe.**

ProcuLink's engine is genuinely good. The delivery layer in particular is better-engineered than most products at any price: atomic claim predicates proven equivalent between C# and Npgsql across a 64-case real-Postgres matrix, a crash-detectable pre-send attempt row, an "unknown outcome" park instead of a guessed re-send on channels that cannot de-duplicate, four independent backstop sweeps with bounded requeue budgets, and claim-first idempotency on all three pull channels. Org scoping holds across every query site I could enumerate. The format catalogue is honest by construction and throws at build time on a typo'd id. That is real, verifiable engineering.

The product is not coherent. Three of the six things the navigation sells as configurable — Output templates, Rules, and Webhook ingress — are wired to nothing. A customer can spend an afternoon in each, get success toasts, and change zero behaviour. Two more (validation/acceptance enforcement, revision pinning) exist in the backend but are disabled or browser-only in production. And the one capability that most differentiates ProcuLink — designing the exact bytes a supplier requires — cannot be reused across orders, and cannot emit a ship-to address at all.

Is it close to market-leading for outbound PO processing? No. It is close to being the only self-service product in a structurally empty niche, which is a better position than "market-leading" and is being squandered on breadth. The wedge is real: across ~20 researched vendors, none has buyer-side outbound PO transformation as its primary job, and only 6 publish a price at all.

Is it sellable today? To a design partner who tolerates founder involvement, yes. To a self-service buyer, no — because at least four screens they will click will lie to them, and two marketing/legal pages make claims the business cannot keep.

## 2. What I believe ProcuLink is

**One sentence.** ProcuLink turns whatever purchase-order file a buyer's system produces into the exact document and delivery channel each individual supplier demands, and keeps the per-supplier knowledge so the next order is cheaper than the last.

**One paragraph.** A procurement team sends POs to dozens of suppliers. Each supplier wants a different thing: this one needs cXML to an Ariba endpoint, that one needs a CSV over SFTP with their own item codes, a third only reads email attachments. Today a coordinator does that reformatting by hand, in Excel, per order, and finds out it was wrong when the supplier rejects it. ProcuLink ingests the buyer's file in any of nine formats, normalises it into one order model, translates the buyer's SKUs into the supplier's SKUs from a learned map and the supplier's own catalogue, surfaces only the lines it is not sure about, renders the supplier's exact required output, delivers it over the supplier's channel, retries intelligently when that fails, and keeps a per-attempt evidence trail with the artifact's SHA-256 so you can prove what you sent.

**Primary user.** Purchasing coordinator / order-processing operator — the person who today has the Excel file open.
**Economic buyer.** Procurement operations manager or Head of Procurement in a 20–500-person distributor, wholesaler, or manufacturer. Not IT.
**Supplier-side beneficiary.** The supplier's order-entry clerk, who receives a machine-readable order in their own codes instead of a PDF they must retype. They never log in.
**Painful process it replaces.** Per-order manual reformatting, item-code lookup by memory or spreadsheet, emailing attachments and hoping, and reconstructing "what did we actually send them?" from a sent-items folder.
**Before/after.** Before: 12 minutes and a rejection risk per PO. After: a queue where 8 of 10 orders are already clean and 2 need one click each.

**What ProcuLink is not.** A chatbot. A PDF parser. A P2P suite (no requisition, approval, budget, or invoice matching). An EDI VAN. A marketplace. An iPaaS.

**Narrowest defensible position.** The supplier-output workbench. Not "we read documents with AI" — every IDP vendor says that. The defensible thing is: for one named supplier, an operator can build the exact document that supplier accepts, prove it renders byte-for-byte, version it, and prove what was delivered — without a consultant. Nobody at €149–999/mo does that.

**Why choose it over the alternatives.** Over spreadsheets: the knowledge persists and the audit trail exists. Over custom scripts: no engineer owns it, and a business user can change a mapping. Over an EDI provider (SPS, TrueCommerce, OpenText): those are quote-only, implementation-led, and priced per trading partner — they cannot economically onboard a supplier who just wants a CSV by email. Over Zapier/Make/n8n: those have no canonical order model, no item-code map, no per-attempt evidence, no supplier-format library — you would be rebuilding ProcuLink badly. Over Boomi/MuleSoft: an order of magnitude more money and a developer.

**One inconvenient finding you should sit with.** The refutation pass on the market track surfaced this: the only two real customer purchase orders in the repository — added on `origin/main` in `63b89b5` (#73) — are `real-cxml-1.2-ariba-punchout-mpn-differs.xml` ("Ariba / KSB → Markit") and `real-cxml-1.1-mpn-equals-supplier-part.xml`. Both are orders **received by** the ProcuLink user, not sent by them. Your own test corpus describes an inbound, supplier-side workflow. Either the fixtures are mislabelled convenience data, or your actual first customer's job is the mirror image of the documented ICP. That is worth resolving before another six weeks of outbound-only work, and I could not resolve it from the repos.

## 3. Whose life it improves

**Procurement / order-processing operator.**
*Disappears:* per-order reformatting; item-code lookup (resolving a line on the review screen writes a reusable `ItemMapping`, so the next order from that supplier auto-resolves it — this is real and on by default `[verified]`); rekeying into a supplier portal.
*Stays theirs:* which supplier an ambiguous order belongs to; whether an AI-suggested SKU is right; whether to send despite a warning.
*New work ProcuLink accidentally creates:* rebuilding the output design for every single order, because a designed structure is never promoted to the supplier `[unverified — refuter cut off, but I confirmed the mechanism myself: grep OutputTree PromoteMappingService.cs = 0]`. Also configuring rules that never fire, and maintaining templates nothing reads.
*Needs to trust it:* the delivered bytes, downloadable. They cannot get them — `getDownloadUrl` exists in `api-client.ts:905` and has zero callers outside its own definition.
*Would make them stop:* one silently wrong price. The XLSX text-cell path (`XlsxOrderParser.cs:149-151`) still uses `NumberStyles.Any` + `InvariantCulture`, so a text-formatted EU price "73,22" becomes 7322.00 with no flag — the one parser of eight that does not route through `NumberParsing` `[verified P1; reachability contested — the LLM extractor is the primary XLSX path]`.

**Supplier-onboarding / integration specialist.**
*Disappears:* writing a transform script per supplier; hand-testing endpoints (the 4-step delivery stepper with a real test-fire is genuinely self-service `[verified]`).
*New work:* discovering by trial that "Output templates" does nothing; that error-severity acceptance rules do not block on the server; that the promote button the help docs tell them to click does not exist in the UI.
*Would make them stop:* the ceiling. `MappedTransformService` exposes 10 header + 11 line canonical names to any custom output. The entity has ~45 header and ~25 line fields. No ShipTo, BillTo, Contact, Incoterms, ManufacturerPartNumber, or UNSPSC can be emitted by a custom mapping or tree — I verified this by reading the row dictionaries at `MappedTransformService.cs:321-336` and `:394-412`. For a physical PO, ship-to is not optional.

**Procurement operations manager.**
*Gets:* a queue-first dashboard with pipeline counts and a blockers jump — genuinely good, not a vanity wire diagram. Per-attempt provenance. A real delivery log.
*Does not get:* trustworthy numbers. `BridgeDashboard.tsx:643-644` computes `countReady = sumStatuses("ready","ready_to_deliver")`; `InboxView.tsx:433` maps the identically-labelled "Ready to send" chip to `ready_to_deliver` only. Two different numbers under the same label on adjacent screens `[verified — refuter failed on four angles]`.
*Would make them stop:* discovering the rules they configured never ran.

**IT / security administrator.**
*Gets:* AES-256-GCM channel credentials with a startup guard that refuses the all-zero placeholder key in production; SSRF protection that defeats DNS rebinding and redirect-to-metadata and is wired into every tenant-URL client including the AWS S3 SDK; HMAC with a ±300 s window, nonce replay cache, and constant-time compare; API keys HMAC-hashed with a server-side secret; org-prefixed blobs and 15-minute signed URLs; GDPR erase that actually deletes R2 objects. This is a strong security posture and I verified the org-scoping claim independently — of 207 `FirstOrDefault`/`SingleOrDefault` sites in Api+Infrastructure only 7 use an Id-only predicate, and all 7 are safe.
*Will object to:* no SSH host-key verification anywhere — a repo-wide grep for `HostKey|known_hosts|ed25519|HostKeyAlgorithm` across `origin/main` returns zero hits, on both SFTP delivery and SFTP pull `[verified]`. An in-path attacker captures POs and credentials. Also: no RLS backstop, one cross-tenant test covering 1 of 87 id-routed endpoints, and PO line text egressing to `api.openai.com` in the US (`appsettings.Production.json` sets `Ai:Provider=openai`; no endpoint override exists in the repo) `[verified P2]`.

**Supplier receiving the order.**
*Gets:* a machine-readable order in their own item codes over their own channel. Genuinely better than a PDF.
*Gets wrongly:* cXML, UBL and X12 artifacts delivered as `application/octet-stream` named `PO-xxx.dat`, because `DeliveryService` re-derives the content type from the format string and its switch only knows xml/json/csv `[verified P1]`. Many receivers reject on content-type or extension. Over SFTP/FTPS the filename is the bare PO number with overwrite enabled `[verified P1]` — two orders with the same PO number silently clobber.

**Verdict on organisation.** The product is organised around its implementation, not these jobs. Six top-level nav items; only "Inbox" names something an operator does. "Rules & formats" is a bucket of four unrelated engines, two of which are inert.

**Competing sources of truth in the data model:**

- `canonical_json` is three things at once: legacy sync-path header, the per-order output design (`OrderMappingOverrideReader.CanonicalKey = "mappingOverride"`), and resolution-path header mirrors. - Supplier configuration lives in six tables with no owner `[unverified]`.

**Where complexity produces bugs rather than value.** `OrderIngestionService` is 2,857 lines doing parsing, normalisation, mapping, item-code resolution, AI, catalog grounding and routing, with a hand-written `new()` composition root of nullable optional dependencies. That shape is exactly what shipped supplier auto-detect dead to production for weeks (fixed in `3db986f`, guarded for ingestion only — still unguarded for transform).

**Plain answers.**

- **Can users design outputs flexibly enough?** For a JSON or CSV supplier, on a single order, yes — and the paste-a-sample inference is genuinely clever (deterministic, no AI, no egress, correctly identifies the per-line repeating group). For anything else, no. The hard ceilings are: (a) the designed structure dies with the order; (b) cXML/UBL/X12 structure cannot be authored at all; (c) the engine's canonical row exposes 10 of ~45 header fields, so no ship-to address can be emitted by any custom output; (d) no reordering; (e) no string/arithmetic transforms inside the designer.
- **Where does it expose internal concepts?** `IncludeWhen` Scriban predicates. Hand-typed namespace URIs. The `src::` token namespace. The word "tree".
- **Where does it pretend to be visual while needing a developer?** Conditionals, namespaces, and anything requiring a manipulator — all reachable only by dropping to Scriban.
- **What is outright impossible?** Reusing a design. Emitting a delivery address. Emitting a typed JSON number. Controlling CSV quoting or line endings. Authoring a valid cXML/UBL/X12 envelope visually.
- **What should stay visual?** The tree, the paste-to-infer, the live preview, the format presets. These work.
- **What should be a structured property editor?** Conditionals ("include this when [field] [is/is not/is empty] [value]"). Namespaces (preset dropdown: UBL 2.1, cXML 1.2, Peppol BIS 3, custom). CSV dialect (delimiter, quote, encoding, line ending). X12 ISA/GS identity — the backend is already there.
- **Where does raw editing belong?** Exactly where it is: Scriban as a labelled advanced mode with a tester. That part is right.
- **How should AI help without taking control?** It already has the correct posture for inference — deterministic, no egress, propose-then-confirm. Extend the same posture: given a pasted sample and a real order, propose per-node bindings with confidence, never auto-apply. Do not let AI author conditionals or namespaces.

**The three changes that would fix the category:**

1. Add `OutputTree` to `PoMappingConfig` (additive JSONB, no migration — same pattern `Output` already uses), promote it in `PromoteMappingService`, consume it in `TryReadSupplierPromotedOutputAsync`, snapshot it into the revision. **This is the single highest-value change in the product.**
2. Widen the `MappedTransformService` header/line row dictionaries to the full canonical field set. Mechanically small, unblocks ship-to.
3. Delete `/library/templates` and point "What we send them" at the supplier's promoted output.

## 9. Architecture and reliability verdict

**Strengths — these are real and I verified the strongest ones.**
Every stage transition is an atomic predicated `ExecuteUpdateAsync` treating 0 rows as "someone else has it". The parse persist is genuinely all-or-nothing. Delivery commits a `dispatching` attempt row with a deterministic idempotency key before the network write, so a crash is detectable and a channel that cannot de-duplicate gets parked rather than re-sent. Claim sets are centralised in one `DeliveryClaim` factory with each delta's product reason documented and test-pinned — the four-hand-written-status-lists bug class is genuinely closed. Concurrency is proven on real Postgres: 64-case claim-equivalence matrix, concurrent dispatch, concurrent retry, crashed-holder recovery, the billing-release/webhook race. Four independent backstop sweeps with bounded requeue budgets. All three pull channels dedupe claim-first against a unique index with documented resume. Docker-dependent tests skip visibly — 155 real-Postgres attributes across 47 classes all use the static-skip attribute. Worker liveness is externally monitored and fails a scheduled probe rather than hiding behind HTTP 200. AI egress has a single enforced chokepoint; a no-egress org never sends line data or PDFs to OpenAI on any ingress path.

**Fragility.**

- `OrderIngestionService` at 2,857 lines, hand-wired with nullable optional dependencies. This is the recurring-bug epicentre; clustering `git log --grep=fix` puts routing/ingestion at the top, and the auto-detect-shipped-dead incident is its signature failure.
- Deterministic parsers fill 4 of ~34 canonical order fields. Every address, contact and total is AI-only — while the outbound emitters are built to carry them `[verified P1]`. That is a structural mismatch between what the model can hold and what the deterministic path can fill.
- The canonical model is not lossless for PDF or email — the two formats where loss matters most. `SourceCapture.TokensJson` works for CSV/XLSX/XML/EDI/X12 and is test-pinned; for PDF the tokenizer returns empty and `raw_text` is hardcoded null at the only call site, so the only surviving unknown-field bag is whatever the LLM chose to emit.
- Revision authority off in production makes the whole versioning subsystem inert there. **→ REFUTED, see the banner.**
- Postmark PUSH inbound email has no message-id or content dedupe — the IMAP PULL path does. REST ingress idempotency is check-then-create, not an atomic claim — concurrent duplicates create two orders.
- `POST /api/orders/{id}/resolve` has no status guard and performs transitions both state maps declare impossible.
- Any 4xx dead-ends the order. `DeliveryService.cs:764-772` classifies every 400–499 as `RejectedBySupplier`; `OrderStatusMachine.cs:99` gives that status no outgoing edges. An expired API key (401) or a moved URL (404) is permanently unrecoverable from the product `[verified P0]`.

**Extensibility.** Parser dispatch is a genuine registry with content sniffing that restores stream position — adding a format is bounded. Output emitters are an AST + emitter set — also bounded. The growing-conditional risk is concentrated in `OrderIngestionService`'s selection ladder and `OrderTransformService`'s six-source precedence method, not in the format layer.

**Observability.** `/health/ready` is genuinely good (I called it — DB, migrations, storage, worker, with heartbeat age). `/operations/health` uses plain language — "New uploads may wait until processing restarts." Credit. But every `/operations/health` deep link is inert because `InboxView` never reads the status query param, and the Worker-outage stall escalation was built then orphaned on a route nothing links to.

**Test strategy, honestly.** 1096 frontend + 1221 Transform verified green by me; ~3,888 backend on CI per STATUS. The suite is real behaviour testing — zero snapshot assertions across 105 frontend files. Two structural holes:

1. CI never runs vitest. `.github/workflows/ci.yml` has exactly two jobs: `build` and `test-e2e`. `bun run test` and `bun run lint` appear nowhere. 105 test files / 1096 assertions gate nothing `[verified P1]`. A ~15-line fix.
2. Every live-transport test is an env-gated silent `return` that CI counts as PASSED `[verified P1]` — including `Live_ImapIngress`, dead since `de4ea0e`, hidden by "2 skipped". SFTP, FTPS and ERP outbound have no happy-path test and no production proof. 26 of ~85 Playwright tests are double-gated and never run in CI, and one skips itself mid-body.

Mock/real divergence is a live liability: `mockUploadPurchaseOrder` never returns `parsing`, never returns `unrouted`, and never throws 400/413/429 — the entire upload failure surface has zero CI coverage. `mockTransformOrder` teleports past `ready_to_deliver` and `delivering`, so the transform→deliver boundary customers actually hit is never exercised.

**No rewrite is warranted.** The boundaries that matter — parser registry, output AST, delivery claim — are sound. The problem is unfinished wiring and one oversized service.

## 18. Final recommendation

**Selectively redesign three areas; do not simplify the engine, and stop adding surface.**

The honest answer to your closing question: ProcuLink's growing technical power has made the product harder to understand and operate, but not harder to trust at the layer where it counts. The delivery engine, the parsers, the security posture, and the routing UX are genuinely good work and should be left alone. What has gone wrong is that the shipping surface grew faster than the wiring behind it. There are now at least four screens where an operator does work that has no effect — Output templates, Rules, and (below 1024 px, and server-side always) validation — plus a designer whose output evaporates on the next order. Every one of those is a trust event, and trust events in procurement software are terminal: an operator who discovers their blocking rule did not block does not file a ticket, they go back to Excel.

So: three redesigns, in this order.

1. **Make authored output reusable and complete** (B1–B3, B12). This is the wedge. Nothing else you could build differentiates you as much, and the enabling change — `OutputTree` on `PoMappingConfig` — is additive with no migration.
2. **Make configured rules actually enforce** (B4–B5, B8). Either the rules engine gates delivery on the server or the screens come out. Both are acceptable; shipping both a rules UI and no evaluator is not.
3. **Cut the taught vocabulary from ~50 nouns to 9** and reorganise nav around Orders / Suppliers / Activity / Settings. This is the largest item and the one most likely to be deferred forever, which is why it should be scheduled, not hoped for.

Around those, do the cheap high-leverage things immediately: B9 (CI runs the tests you already wrote — 15 lines, protects 1096 assertions), B7 (content type and filename — many receivers reject `.dat` outright), B6 (4xx exit), and B11 (two marketing/legal sentences that are currently false). And run the two one-command checks in §17 that could flip a P0 either way.

Then stop. No invoices, no Peppol, no PunchOut, no fourth untested channel. You are the only self-service product in an empty niche; the risk is not that a competitor takes it, it is that you spread across enough surface that none of it is finishable.

## 1. Executive verdict

**Partially — with conditions, one of them severe.**

The *engine* is good. The delivery layer is better-engineered than most products at any price:
atomic claim predicates proven equivalent between C# and Npgsql across a 64-case real-Postgres
matrix; a crash-detectable pre-send attempt row; an "unknown outcome" park instead of a guessed
re-send on channels that cannot de-duplicate; four backstop sweeps with bounded requeue budgets;
claim-first idempotency on all three pull channels. Org scoping holds across every query site.

The *product* is not coherent. Three surfaces the navigation sells as configurable — **Output
templates**, **Rules**, **Webhook ingress** — are wired to nothing. Two more (acceptance enforcement,
revision pinning) exist in the backend but were believed disabled or are browser-only. And the one
capability that differentiates ProcuLink — designing the exact bytes a supplier requires — could not
be reused across orders and could not emit a ship-to address.

Sellable to a design partner today. Not to a self-service buyer: at least four screens they will
click will lie to them, and two marketing pages make claims the business cannot keep.

## 2. What ProcuLink is

**One sentence.** ProcuLink turns whatever purchase-order file a buyer's system produces into the
exact document and delivery channel each individual supplier demands, and keeps the per-supplier
knowledge so the next order is cheaper than the last.

**Primary user:** purchasing coordinator. **Economic buyer:** procurement ops manager at a 20–500
person distributor/wholesaler/manufacturer — not IT. **Supplier-side beneficiary:** the supplier's
order-entry clerk, who never logs in.

**Not:** a chatbot, a PDF parser, a P2P suite, an EDI VAN, a marketplace, an iPaaS.

**Narrowest defensible position — the supplier-output workbench.** Not "we read documents with AI";
every IDP vendor says that. The defensible claim is: *for one named supplier, an operator can build
the exact document that supplier accepts, prove it renders byte-for-byte, version it, and prove what
was delivered — without a consultant.* Nobody at €149–999/mo does that.

**⚠ Unresolved and material.** The only two real customer POs in the repo —
`real-cxml-1.2-ariba-punchout-mpn-differs.xml` ("Ariba / KSB → Markit") and `real-cxml-1.1-mpn-equals-supplier-part.xml`, both
added in `63b89b5` — are orders **received by** the ProcuLink user, not sent by them. Either
convenience fixtures, or the first real customer's job is the mirror of the documented ICP. Resolve
before WP-25/WP-26 rewrite the vocabulary around "outbound".

## 9. Architecture and reliability verdict

**Strengths (verified).** Atomic predicated `ExecuteUpdateAsync` at every stage transition, 0 rows
treated as "someone else has it". All-or-nothing parse persist. Dispatch attempt row with a
deterministic idempotency key committed **before** the network write. One `DeliveryClaim` factory
consumed by all five gates, each delta's reason documented and test-pinned. Concurrency proven on
real Postgres: 64-case claim matrix, concurrent dispatch/retry, crashed-holder recovery,
billing-release vs webhook race. 155 real-Postgres attributes across 47 classes all skip **visibly**.
Worker liveness externally monitored. A single enforced AI-egress chokepoint.

**Fragility.** `OrderIngestionService` is **2,857 lines** doing parse + normalise + map + item-code +
AI + catalog + routing, hand-wired with nullable optional dependencies — this is the recurring-bug
epicentre and produced the auto-detect-shipped-dead incident. Deterministic parsers fill **4 of ~34**
canonical fields; every address, contact and total is AI-only `[verified P1]`. The canonical model is
**not lossless for PDF or email**. Postmark inbound has no dedupe (the IMAP path does). REST ingress
idempotency is check-then-create, not an atomic claim. `POST /orders/{id}/resolve` has no status
guard. **Any 4xx dead-ends** — `DeliveryService.cs:764-772` + `OrderStatusMachine.cs:99` (no outgoing
edges) `[verified]`.

**Test strategy, honestly.** 1096 FE + 1221 Transform verified green first-hand; ~3,888 BE on CI.
Zero snapshot assertions across 105 FE files — real behaviour testing. Two structural holes:
**CI runs neither vitest nor lint** `[verified]`, and **every live-transport test is an env-gated
silent `return` that CI counts as PASSED** `[verified]`.

**No rewrite warranted.** Parser registry, output AST and delivery claim are sound boundaries. The
problem is unfinished wiring and one oversized service.

### 11b. EU residency — hardened 2026-07-30, and worse than sections 6 and 8 said

The audit called this "overstated: four US subprocessors, and PO text to `api.openai.com`". Both true,
both understating it. Three further facts, each verified first-hand:

1. **Postmark carries the OUTBOUND PURCHASE ORDER, not just inbound mail.**
   `EmailApiDeliveryDispatcher.cs:11` — "Sends the generated artifact as an email"; `:109` attaches it
   (`Attachments: new[] { new EmailApiAttachment(...) }`); its test asserts the attachment filename is
   `PO-1.xml`. `email` is an offered, live-proven delivery channel. **A US subprocessor receives the
   complete purchase-order document.** Every version of the residency copy — the current one AND the
   replacement this plan originally proposed — calls the US email category "inbound email". That is
   materially wrong, and it propagates into `/dpa`, which incorporates the subprocessor list by reference.
2. **`EGRESS-GEO` was raised by our own pre-launch QA and never resolved.**
   `docs/qa/2026-06-29-prelaunch-audit-and-test-plan.md:1065` records the outbound delivery POST egressing
   from **`152.55.184.78` (Durham, NC, US)**, noting "a supplier's server logs will show a US source IP";
   `:1090` files it again as open. `git grep EGRESS-GEO origin/main` returns those two lines and nothing else.
3. **The EU evidence base is thinner than the claim.** `R2Endpoint` is **empty** at
   `appsettings.Production.json:21`. Nothing in either repo records the Neon region. The only in-repo
   support for "Railway europe-west4" is `docs/qa/2026-06-29-...:193`, which cites
   `src/lib/subprocessors.ts` — **the page citing itself.**

**This invalidates part of this plan's own prescription, which is the more important lesson.** WP-10 as
originally written told the implementer to assert "order files and the database are EU-region (Neon, R2,
Railway europe-west4)" — replacing a vague false claim with a **more specific and more confident** one,
against the exact region our own QA had flagged as contradicted, and naming a label that does not match
the provider (`europe-west4` is a GCP name; Neon runs on AWS). WP-10 is rewritten: **measure and record
the actual regions before writing any sentence that asserts one.** A fix that makes a false claim more
precise is worse than the claim it replaces.

## 14. Final recommendation

Selectively redesign three areas; do not simplify the engine; stop adding surface.

ProcuLink's growing technical power has made the product harder to understand and operate, but not
harder to trust at the layer where it counts. The delivery engine, parsers, security posture and
routing UX are good work — leave them alone. What went wrong is that the shipping surface grew faster
than the wiring behind it, and trust events in procurement software are terminal: an operator who
discovers their blocking rule did not block goes back to Excel.

1. Make authored output reusable and complete (WP-12 → WP-16).
2. Make configured rules actually enforce (WP-07, WP-17, WP-18, or remove the screens).
3. Cut the taught vocabulary to 9 nouns and reorganise nav to four items (WP-25, WP-26).

Around those, the cheap high-leverage items: WP-01 (CI runs the tests you already wrote), WP-20
(content type and filename), WP-19 (4xx exit), WP-10 (two false sentences).

Then stop. No invoices, no Peppol, no PunchOut, no fourth untested channel. You are the only
self-service product in an empty niche; the risk is not that a competitor takes it, but that you
spread across enough surface that none of it is finishable.

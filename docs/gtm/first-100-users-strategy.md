# ProcuLink — First 100 Customers Strategy

_Last updated: 2026-05-28. Solo founder, pre-revenue, Baltic/EU SME procurement. Months tracked from "Group J live QA passes" — i.e. the day Pilot #1 can actually run on a deployed Railway + Vercel stack._

---

## Phase 1 — 0 to 10 customers (months 1–4)

**Channel:** Warm network only. LinkedIn 1st/2nd connections, former colleagues, Estonian/Latvian/Lithuanian procurement Slack/WhatsApp groups, e-Residency community, 1-2 procurement consultants who already work with Erply/Directo customers.

**Mechanic:** Founder hand-delivers every Pilot. Demo → 14-day Pilot → convert to Operations (€399/mo).

**Pricing:** €399/month + €500 one-time setup. Setup fee waived for first 5 customers in exchange for a logo and a 1-line testimonial after 60 days of paid use.

**Monthly milestones:**

| Month | New paid | Cumulative paid | Pilots started | Demos done | Outreach touches |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 8 | 60 |
| 2 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 10 | 60 |
| 3 | 3 | 6 | 5 | 12 | 60 |
| 4 | 4 | 10 | 5 | 12 | 60 |

**Channel costs (monthly):** ~€100. LinkedIn Premium (€60), a domain/calendar booking tool (€20), occasional coffee meetings (€20). Effectively zero paid acquisition.

**Success metric:** ≥50% Pilot → paid conversion. Below that, the product is broken or the ICP is wrong — diagnose before scaling.

**Proof points to capture by end of Phase 1:**
- 3+ logos (with permission) for the landing page
- 2+ named testimonials with role + company
- 1 anonymised case study: "How {Company} cut PO processing from 25 min to 3 min"
- Pilot → paid conversion rate (target ≥50%)
- Average time-to-first-delivery in Pilot (target ≤90 minutes founder time)
- Customer-driven order volume in week 2 of Pilot (target ≥5)

**Quit criteria — abandon this phase if:**
- After 30 cold/warm touches and 10 demos, fewer than 2 Pilots started → ICP mismatch; pivot the targeting before pivoting the product.
- Pilot → paid conversion ≤25% after 6 Pilots → the demo is over-promising or the product is under-delivering. Audit which.

## Phase 2 — 10 to 30 customers (months 4–8)

**Channel:** Referral motion + one ERP-consultant partnership.

**Referral mechanic:** Existing customers get one month credit (€399 off) for every referral that converts to a paid plan. Cap: 6 months of credit per year, then it converts to extra orders/suppliers. Don't make it cash — it has to keep them in the product.

**ERP consultant partnership:** Pick one Estonian Erply or Directo implementation consultant. Offer them 20% of year-one revenue per referred customer (so €958 per Operations customer they bring) for the first 10 referrals. This is a one-off acquisition cost; once they see ProcuLink in their installs, the referrals become organic.

**Monthly milestones:**

| Month | New paid | Cumulative paid | Referrals as % of new | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 5 | 4 | 14 | 25% | First referral lands |
| 6 | 4 | 18 | 30% | Consultant partnership active |
| 7 | 5 | 23 | 35% | Referral loop starts compounding |
| 8 | 7 | 30 | 40% | Begin Phase 3 setup work in parallel |

**Channel costs (monthly):** ~€500. Referral credits (€400 once meaningful referrals land), Premium tools (€100). Consultant commission is paid out of revenue, not budget.

**Success metric:** ≥30% of new customers come from referrals or the consultant partnership by month 8. If the product is good, this metric grows on its own.

**Proof points to capture by end of Phase 2:**
- ≥30 named customers with logos
- 1 published case study (long-form, with numbers — "from 25 min/PO to 3 min/PO across 200 orders/month")
- Referral conversion data: which customer types refer the best (size, ERP, country)
- ERP-consultant partnership ROI: revenue attributed vs commission paid
- Net dollar retention (NDR) signal: customers upgrading Growth → Operations or Operations → Integration

**Quit criteria — abandon this phase if:**
- Referrals < 10% of new customers at month 7 → product isn't sticky enough; investigate week-4 churn signals before adding new channels.
- ERP consultant brings 0 referrals in 90 days → either wrong consultant or wrong commercial structure; replace, don't extend.

## Phase 3 — 30 to 100 customers (months 8–18)

**Channel:** Content + paid SEM + first hires.

**Content marketing:** One long-form post per week, focused on specific procurement workflows. Topics: "How to send a PO from Erply to {supplier name}", "Directo PO export quirks every procurement manager hits", "What cXML actually requires from a buyer". Publish to LinkedIn, founder blog, and Baltic procurement communities. Don't try to rank in English globally — own the Baltic Erply/Directo niche.

**Paid SEM:** Target high-intent long-tail keywords. Start budget: €500/month. Keywords:
- "Erply purchase order integration"
- "Directo PO import"
- "Business Central supplier PO automation"
- "cXML Baltics"
- Estonian/Latvian/Lithuanian translations of the above

Don't bid on "procurement software" — the CPC is €15+ and the intent is wrong.

**Hire plan:**
- **Month 11 (≈customer #30):** Hire CSM #1. Part-time at first, then full-time at customer #50. Their job: pilot onboarding, week-1 retention, expansion conversations. Founder stops doing every onboarding personally.
- **Month 15 (≈customer #50):** Hire SDR #1. Their job: cold outreach to a pre-qualified list (the founder maintains the ICP rubric, SDR runs it). 30 booked demos/month target by month 18.

**Monthly milestones:**

| Month | New paid | Cumulative paid | Channel mix | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 9 | 8 | 38 | 50% referral, 30% warm, 20% content | First content posts compounding |
| 10 | 9 | 47 | 50/25/25 | SEM dialing in |
| 11 | 10 | 57 | 45/20/25/10 SEM | CSM #1 starts |
| 12 | 10 | 67 | Same | Christmas slowdown |
| 14 | 11 | 89 | 40/15/25/20 | |
| 15 | 11 | 100 | Same | SDR #1 starts |
| 18 | 12 | 100+ | 30/10/30/30 | Self-serve channels growing |

**Channel costs (monthly, by month 15):** ~€7,500. SEM (€1,500), content tools/freelance editor (€500), CSM salary (€3,500 loaded), SDR salary (€2,000 loaded part-time start). Funded by ARR — at customer #50 ProcuLink should be at ~€20,000 MRR.

**Success metric:** Customer acquisition cost (CAC) ≤ 1.5× monthly Operations price (€600). Payback period ≤ 4 months. Anything worse means the channel mix is wrong.

**Proof points to capture by end of Phase 3:**
- 100+ paying customers
- ≥3 published case studies, ≥10 named testimonials
- Top-3 Google ranking on at least 5 Baltic procurement long-tail keywords
- Documented CAC by channel (referral, content, SEM, SDR-sourced) and channel-specific LTV
- CSM-managed Pilot → paid conversion ≥55% (better than founder-only, because consistency)
- First Enterprise customer (€2,500+/mo) signed
- ≥1 Erply OR Directo OR ERP-vendor co-marketing relationship

**Quit criteria — abandon this phase's channels if:**
- SEM CAC > €1,500 after 90 days of optimisation → kill SEM, double down on referral and content.
- Content posts averaging <500 reads after 8 weeks → kill content, redirect to SDR-led outbound.
- CSM-onboarded Pilots converting worse than founder-onboarded → onboarding is too tribal; codify and re-train, don't hire SDR until conversion recovers.

## Tripwires (kill-or-pivot signals)

These are the explicit signals that mean STOP and re-plan, not push harder.

1. **If month 4 closes with <3 paid customers** → Kill cold outreach. Pause SEM/content plans. Spend the next 30 days exclusively on referrals from the customers you DO have, plus one ERP consultant partnership. Cold outreach isn't working for this ICP and you need to find out why before spending more time on it.

2. **If Pilot → paid conversion drops below 30% across any 3-month window** → STOP signing new Pilots. The funnel is broken. Either the demo is over-promising (audit the demo recording vs the Pilot UX gaps) or week-2 friction is too high (audit support requests and stuck-order states). Fix before re-opening the funnel.

3. **If churn exceeds 5%/month at any point with <50 customers** → Halt all new acquisition. With a small base every churn is meaningful signal. Interview every churner personally. If two cite the same root cause, fix it before adding customer #51.

4. **If 6 months in, no customer has upgraded from Growth (€149) to Operations (€399)** → The pricing/feature gating is wrong. Either Growth is too generous or Operations doesn't have enough delta. Re-price before adding customer #20.

5. **If at customer #30, you cannot answer "what's the one thing that makes ProcuLink un-rip-out-able for our top 5 customers?" in one sentence** → You have no moat. Spend 4 weeks talking to those 5 customers before hiring CSM or SDR. The answer might be auto-learned mappings, the audit trail, the Erply/Directo connectors, or something you haven't shipped yet — but you need to know which.

## Cross-phase reminders

- **Don't hire before customer #30.** Founder-led sales until then. Hiring earlier hides the product problems behind a person.
- **Don't raise outside capital before customer #10.** The terms get worse with every month of zero-revenue. €4,000 MRR (10 paid Operations customers) is a real conversation; €0 MRR is a beg.
- **Don't add a Growth-tier marketing push until Operations conversion is solid.** Growth (€149) is a downgrade path for prospects who can't justify Operations, not a primary acquisition tier. Sell Operations first.
- **Don't open a non-Baltic geography until customer #50.** Estonia + Latvia + Lithuania + Finland is enough TAM for 200 customers at €399/mo. International expansion is a phase-4 problem.
- **Track every Pilot's outcome, including losses.** Lost-Pilot reasons compound into the highest-leverage roadmap signal you'll get for the first 12 months.
